Penguin Techno

A genre-first discovery and ticketing app for Barcelona's underground techno scene.

Role
Sole designer, end to end (personal project)
Tools
Figma (prototyping, motion & interaction design)
Scope
Full user journey — onboarding, discovery, event details, checkout, digital ticket

The problem

Finding and booking tickets to underground and techno events is fragmented. Unlike mainstream festivals, this scene lives across Instagram stories, Resident Advisor listings, venue websites, and word of mouth — there's no single, curated place to discover events by the specific sound you're into, or to book quickly once you find something.

For a scene that moves fast — lineups drop late, tickets sell out, plans are often last-minute — that fragmentation is a real barrier. Penguin Techno was a personal project built to solve this for Barcelona specifically: a focused, genre-driven app for discovering and booking underground events without the noise.

My approach

I designed the full flow end to end, from first open to a usable digital ticket, treating it as a real product rather than a set of unconnected screens.

  • Genre-first onboarding. Instead of a generic "welcome" screen, the app opens by asking users to pick the sub-styles they're into — Industrial, Detroit, Minimal, and others. This immediately personalizes discovery around taste, not just location, which matters more in techno than in mainstream music where genre lines are blurrier.
  • Curated + algorithmic balance on the homepage. The home feed pairs a hand-picked "Penguin Pick" section with a broader "Popular" grid. This mirrors how the scene actually works — trusted curation matters as much as raw popularity, especially for lesser-known artists and DIY venues.
  • Reviews as trust signals. Since many of these events are at smaller, less-known venues, I added a reviews layer so users can gauge an event or venue before committing — filling the trust gap that bigger platforms solve with sheer scale.
  • Low-friction checkout. The booking flow supports both card entry and express wallet payment (Apple Pay / Google Pay–style options), since last-minute, spontaneous ticket purchases are common in this scene — the checkout needed to be fast, not just functional.
  • A practical digital ticket. The final ticket screen includes a QR code, order ID, and venue address together on one screen — designed for the real moment of arriving at a door at night, not just as a confirmation receipt.
  • Motion as part of the experience, not decoration. I prototyped custom transitions and animated states (an animated welcome screen, animated title reveals, interactive button states) — reflecting the same high-energy identity as the events themselves, and letting me demonstrate interaction/motion design alongside UI.

Outcome

Penguin Techno is a personal project, not a shipped product — but it's a complete, coherent flow: onboarding → personalized discovery → event detail → checkout → wallet-ready ticket. It's the project where I pushed hardest on interaction and motion design, and on designing for a real, specific audience (underground scene-goers) rather than a generic use case.
